# User Research

## Overview

This user research section summarizes the findings and observations gathered during the early discovery phase of this project. The goal of this research is to better understand how users currently discover, evaluate, and interact with local restaurants online, particularly when relying on third-party delivery platforms and search engines.

Because this project does not include formal interviews or large-scale surveys, the research is primarily based on competitive analysis, observation of existing platforms, and review of publicly available customer feedback on delivery apps and review sites.

The purpose of this research is not to produce statistical conclusions, but to identify recurring patterns, pain points, and behaviors that can inform design decisions.

---

## Research Methods

To understand the current user experience, the following methods were used:

- **Competitive analysis** of restaurant websites and delivery platforms to understand common patterns in menu presentation, navigation, and ordering flows.
- **Observation of user behavior** on platforms such as DoorDash, Grubhub, and Google Maps listings, focusing on how users access menus and make decisions.
- **Review analysis** of customer feedback from delivery platforms and search listings to identify common frustrations and expectations.
- **Assumption mapping** based on personal experience and typical user behavior when ordering food online.

These methods were used together to form a baseline understanding of user needs and expectations.

---

## Key Observations

### Fragmented User Experience

One of the most consistent observations is that users rarely interact with a restaurant through a single source of truth. Instead, they move between multiple platforms such as Google search results, map listings, and delivery applications.

Each platform presents information differently, which creates inconsistency in menu presentation, pricing, and restaurant branding. This fragmentation makes it harder for users to form a clear understanding of the restaurant before placing an order.

---

### Menu Accessibility Is Critical

Users prioritize menu access above almost all other information when evaluating a restaurant. However, menus are often difficult to locate or poorly presented on third-party platforms.

Common issues include:
- Menus being buried behind multiple navigation steps
- Inconsistent formatting between platforms
- Lack of clear categorization or highlighting of popular items

Users tend to make decisions quickly, so friction in accessing the menu directly impacts whether they proceed with ordering.

---

### Visual Information Strongly Influences Decisions

Food images and visual presentation play a significant role in user decision-making. When images are missing, low quality, or inconsistent, users are less confident in their choices.

In contrast, clear visuals of signature dishes or popular items help users make faster and more confident decisions.

---

### Lack of Restaurant Identity

On delivery platforms, restaurants often appear as a list of items rather than a brand with personality or story. This limits the user's ability to understand what makes a restaurant unique.

Users generally have little exposure to:
- Restaurant background or history
- Signature dishes or specialties
- Values or cultural context

This can reduce emotional connection and brand recognition.

---

### Mobile-First Behavior

Most users interact with restaurant information on mobile devices. This includes searching for restaurants, reading reviews, browsing menus, and placing orders.

As a result, users expect:
- Fast loading experiences
- Simple navigation
- Clear hierarchy of information
- Minimal steps to complete actions

Complex layouts or dense information structures lead to frustration and abandonment.

---

## Key Insights

From these observations, several key insights were identified:

- Users prioritize speed and clarity over depth when browsing restaurant information.
- Menu accessibility is the most critical factor in decision-making.
- Visual presentation significantly impacts trust and perceived quality.
- Users rely heavily on third-party platforms, which limits brand control and consistency.
- A dedicated website can improve both usability and brand perception by centralizing information.

---

## Design Implications

These insights directly inform the design direction of this project:

- The menu must be a primary focus of the website and easily accessible from the homepage.
- Visual hierarchy should prioritize featured and popular dishes to support faster decision-making.
- The interface must be optimized for mobile-first usage with minimal friction between pages.
- The website should include storytelling elements to strengthen brand identity and trust.
- Navigation should be simple, consistent, and focused on key user tasks such as browsing and ordering.

---

## Summary

This research highlights a clear opportunity to improve the restaurant experience by consolidating fragmented information into a single, cohesive digital platform. By addressing issues related to menu accessibility, visual clarity, and brand identity, the redesigned experience aims to better support how users naturally discover and evaluate restaurants online.
