# User Personas

This section defines primary user types for the restaurant website redesign. The personas are based on general assumptions about how people discover and interact with local restaurants online, particularly through mobile devices, search engines, and delivery platforms.

These personas are not based on formal interviews but are intended to guide design decisions, highlight user needs, and ensure the interface supports realistic user goals and behaviors.

---

## Primary Persona: Busy Local Customer

### Name
Hania Amir

### Age
29

### Occupation
Marketing Associate

### Context of Use
Hanua typically orders takeout or delivery after work or on weekends. She often uses her phone to search for nearby restaurants, read reviews, and place orders quickly with minimal friction.

She discovers restaurants through Google Maps, word of mouth, or delivery apps, and expects fast access to menus, pricing, and ordering options.

---

### Goals

Hania’s main goal is to decide what to eat quickly and confidently without spending too much time searching across multiple apps or websites. She wants to be able to view the menu clearly, identify popular dishes, and understand what the restaurant is known for.

She also wants to avoid confusion caused by inconsistent or outdated information across different platforms.

---

### Behaviors

- Uses mobile phone as primary device for restaurant discovery and ordering
- Relies on visuals (photos, ratings, highlighted dishes) to make decisions
- Prefers familiar ordering platforms but is open to direct ordering if it is simple
- Skims information quickly rather than reading long descriptions
- Often compares multiple restaurants before making a decision

---

### Pain Points

- Difficulty finding a clean, easy-to-read menu in one place
- Frustration with inconsistent information across delivery apps and search results
- Lack of insight into what dishes are popular or recommended
- Limited understanding of the restaurant’s identity or story
- Slow or cluttered interfaces that make decision-making harder

---

### Needs

- A clear and well-structured menu that is easy to browse on mobile
- Highlighted popular or recommended dishes to reduce decision fatigue
- Simple navigation to key information such as hours, location, and ordering
- A sense of trust and clarity about the restaurant’s quality and offerings
- A fast path from discovery to ordering without unnecessary steps

---

## Secondary Persona: Curious First-Time Visitor

### Name
Bilal Abbas Khan

### Age
34

### Occupation
Software Engineer

### Context of Use
Bilal is exploring new restaurants in his area. He is not necessarily looking to order immediately but wants to learn more about the restaurant before deciding whether it is worth visiting or ordering from.

He tends to value information, credibility, and presentation when evaluating new places.

---

### Goals

Bilal wants to understand what makes the restaurant unique, what type of food it offers, and whether it aligns with his preferences. He is interested in exploring the menu, reading about the restaurant’s background, and viewing photos of dishes.

---

### Behaviors

- Searches online before deciding where to eat
- Spends more time evaluating options compared to quick-order users
- Reads menus in detail and compares pricing and variety
- Looks for signs of quality such as design, reviews, and presentation
- May switch between desktop and mobile depending on context

---

### Pain Points

- Poorly structured or hard-to-read menus
- Lack of context about the restaurant’s identity or specialties
- Generic or unappealing digital presentation
- Difficulty understanding what dishes are recommended or signature items

---

### Needs

- A visually appealing and easy-to-navigate website
- Clear presentation of menu categories and signature dishes
- Restaurant story or background to build trust and interest
- High-quality visuals of food items
- Simple access to location and contact details

---

## Summary

These personas represent two key user groups: one focused on speed and convenience, and another focused on exploration and evaluation. The design should support both by balancing fast access to key actions with enough depth to build trust and interest in the restaurant.
